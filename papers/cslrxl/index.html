CSLR-XL: Scaling Continuous Sign Language Recognition with Synthetic Pretraining

Karahan Sahin1,  Ozge Mercanoglu Sincan1,  Edward Fish2,  Richard Bowden1

1CVSSP, University of Surrey, United Kingdom  ·  2Tavus

Abstract

Continuous sign language recognition (CSLR) is a crucial task in sign language understanding, providing the temporally ordered, sign-level supervision that downstream tasks such as translation can build upon. Unlike sign language translation, which maps signing to spoken-language text, CSLR focuses on aligning the visual stream to its underlying sequence of signs. Yet its applications remain constrained by the scarcity of annotated data. In this work, we present CSLR-XL, a framework that addresses these gaps through synthetic pretraining and visually grounded pseudo-glossing. We introduce SignSynth-X, a large-scale synthetic corpus of densely annotated continuous pose sequences produced by stitching sign spottings sourced from continuous video. Unlike prior isolated-dictionary stitching, our method approximates natural co-articulation between signs, enabling the learning of sign priors from synthetic data. To demonstrate the value of the resulting synthetic sequences, we train a multi-stream skeleton-based recogniser. Relative to the MSKA recogniser, our EHM representation and synthetic pretraining together reduce test WER by 3.6 points on CSL-Daily and 5.2 on MeineDGS, of which synthetic pretraining alone contributes 2.6 and 2.5, respectively. The model remains competitive with skeleton-based methods on Phoenix2014T. We further introduce a force-aligned CTC pseudo-glosser, trained only on synthetic data, that scores complete candidate gloss sequences against the visual stream. On Phoenix2014T, it reduces pseudo-gloss WER from 60.9 to 41.8 compared with the strongest prior baseline, an absolute improvement of 19.1 points and a relative reduction of 31%. SignSynth-X and our code will be made publicly available.
