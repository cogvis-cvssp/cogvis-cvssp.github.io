SignSeek: Learning Transferable Representations for Sign Dictionary Retrieval

Sobhan Asasi1,  Ozge Mercanoglu Sincan1,  Richard Bowden1

1CVSSP, University of Surrey, United Kingdom

Paper arXiv Code Coming soon

Sign dictionary retrieval with SignSeek. Given only a query video of an unknown sign, SignSeek ranks the entries of a sign dictionary by embedding similarity and returns the top matches, with no fine-tuning on the dictionary.

Abstract

Sign language dictionaries are essential resources for sign language learners, yet automatically retrieving a sign from a dictionary, given only a query video, remains a challenging problem due to the natural variability between signers. Existing sign representation learning methods are built for closed-set recognition, producing embeddings that do not generalise to the open-set, signer-independent setting that retrieval demands. SignSeek closes this gap by contrastively learning sign representations with saliency-guided articulator masking. A contrastive objective aligns same-gloss signs across signers, while our Articulator Saliency-Guided Masking (ASGM) pinpoints the single most critical articulator per sign. This drives two complementary objectives, a masked contrastive alignment (MAC) loss that sees the sign through a single articulator and a masked prediction (MAP) loss that reconstructs it in latent space from the surrounding spatio-temporal context. Pretrained on 266K samples (~5,700 glosses) across multiple sign languages, SignSeek sets a new state-of-the-art performance in cross-corpus retrieval on ASL-Citizen, WLASL, and NMFs-CSL without any downstream fine-tuning. Strikingly, it achieves zero-shot generalisation to an entirely unseen British Sign Language (BSL), surpassing methods explicitly trained on BSL, and transfers seamlessly to isolated sign recognition and subtitle alignment, outperforming prior skeleton-based methods.

Method

Fig. 1 — Overview of SignSeek. A balanced-sample batch of query and reference clips is encoded per articulator by the Articulatory Graph Encoder, fused by a Conformer and pooled into a single embedding. GAC contrasts intact embeddings, MAC aligns a query seen only through its dominant articulator with clean same-gloss references, and MAP predicts the clean representation of a clip whose dominant articulator is masked. ASGM selects that dominant articulator per clip.

Articulatory Backbone: Pose In, One Embedding Out

Per-Articulator Graph Encoders 2D keypoints are split into body, face, left hand and right hand. Each stream is encoded by its own adaptive graph network; the two hands share an encoder, with the right hand mirrored so both live in a common canonical frame.
Temporal Fusion Encoder The concatenated articulator streams are fused by a Conformer and pooled by attention into a single L2-normalised clip embedding, used directly for retrieval by cosine similarity.

ASGM: Articulator Saliency-Guided Masking

Which Articulator Carries the Sign? Saliency is read from the encoder itself: the norm of each articulator's standardised, temporally pooled embedding. No auxiliary supervision and no learned parameters.
Gumbel-Max Selection A temperature-scaled softmax turns saliency into a distribution, and one dominant articulator is sampled per clip, keeping the saliency ordering in expectation while preserving variety.

Three Objectives: Through and Without the Dominant Articulator

Global Articulator Contrast (GAC) A supervised contrastive loss over intact clips pulls same-gloss signs from different signers together, shaping the signer-invariant space that retrieval demands.
Masked Articulator Contrast (MAC) Keeps only the dominant articulator of the query and aligns it with clean same-gloss references, forcing the most salient articulator alone to be gloss-discriminative.
Masked Articulator Prediction (MAP) Masks the dominant articulator and predicts the clean Conformer representation in latent space from the surrounding context, capturing the structural dependencies between articulators.
